Business email compromise attempt

03/03/2026 · 9:00 AM

Description · The accounts payable team at a mid-sized architecture firm flagged an email requesting an urgent change to a vendor's payment routing number. The message impersonated a known supplier and was investigated as a suspected business email compromise (BEC) attempt.

Tools used · Email header analyser (message trace with SPF, DKIM and DMARC results), domain age lookup (WHOIS).

The 5 W's · An external actor spoofed the domain of a long-standing HVAC supplier from a look-alike domain registered nine days earlier, with the digit "1" substituted for the letter "l". The email arrived at 8:47 AM in the accounts payable inbox on the corporate Microsoft 365 tenant and was flagged by the clerk nine minutes later. No payment was released, so no compromise beyond the phishing attempt itself occurred.

Additional notes

Header analysis: SPF = fail, DKIM = none. Domain registered via a privacy-proxy registrar 9 days prior.

Actions taken: blocked sender domain at the mail gateway, notified the real supplier out-of-band, added a payment-change verification step (call-back to a known number) to the AP procedure.

Open question: should look-alike domain monitoring be added for the top 20 vendors?

Ransomware via exposed RDP

04/18/2026 · 2:00 AM

Description · A file-encrypting ransomware variant was detected on two servers at a regional logistics company after an exposed remote desktop (RDP) port was brute-forced. The case covers containment through recovery.

Tools used · EDR alert console, firewall and VPN logs, offline backup restore procedure.

The 5 W's · An unattributed actor gained access via credential-stuffing against an internet-facing RDP endpoint (port 3389 left open on a warehouse management server). After roughly 40 minutes of repeated login attempts starting at 1:31 AM, the attacker authenticated with a weak local admin password, disabled the endpoint agent and deployed a payload that encrypted shared drives on two on-premises Windows servers. Encryption activity triggered an EDR alert at 2:14 AM. The port had been left exposed without MFA or IP allowlisting after a vendor's temporary remote-support setup was never rolled back.

Additional notes

Containment: isolated both hosts from the network, closed RDP at the perimeter firewall, rotated all local admin credentials.

Recovery: restored affected shares from the previous night's offline backup (4-hour data loss window). No ransom paid.

Follow-up: audit all firewall rules for other temporary vendor-access exceptions; require MFA on any remote administration path going forward.

Departing employee data removal

05/22/2026 · 4:00 PM

Description · A departing employee at a software company copied a customer contract archive to a personal USB drive three days before their resignation date. The case was reviewed for policy violation and closed out with process recommendations.

Tools used · DLP (data loss prevention) alert log, endpoint USB device history, HR offboarding checklist.

The 5 W's · A sales operations employee who had submitted their resignation but remained on payroll for a two-week transition period triggered a DLP rule with a bulk transfer of a shared drive folder containing signed customer contracts to an unregistered USB device at 4:05 PM, three business days before their last day, on a company-issued laptop on-site. USB storage was not blocked by policy for the role, and departing-employee monitoring was not yet in place until the final offboarding day.

Additional notes

Resolution: HR and legal were looped in; the employee confirmed and deleted the copy under supervision as a condition of a clean separation.

Process gap: departing-employee monitoring should begin at resignation notice, not on the last working day.

Recommendation: block removable storage by default for roles with access to customer contract data, with exceptions granted case-by-case.

Misconfigured cloud storage bucket

06/09/2026 · 12:00 PM

Description · A misconfigured cloud storage bucket belonging to a health-and-wellness app was found publicly listable by an external security researcher, who reported it through the company's disclosure programme before any confirmed misuse.

Tools used · Cloud provider access logs, bucket policy and IAM review, responsible-disclosure intake ticket.

The 5 W's · No malicious actor was identified. The exposure was traced to an internal engineering change that set a storage bucket's access policy to public during a migration, caught and reported responsibly before confirmed misuse.

Why this entry matters

Detection and analysis is not only about attackers. Configuration drift during routine engineering work is one of the most common causes of real-world data exposure.

The journal applies the same 5 W's discipline whether the cause is an adversary, an insider or an honest mistake.

Botium Toys Security Audit

Scenario

Botium Toys is a small fictional U.S. toy business with a single physical site acting as office, storefront and warehouse. Its online presence has grown internationally, and the IT manager commissioned an internal audit to secure infrastructure, identify risks to critical assets and check compliance obligations around online payments and E.U. business.

Scope and goals

The entire security programme: company equipment and devices, the internal network and systems. Goals included reviewing controls against expectations, conducting a risk assessment, completing control assessments, assessing compliance and communicating results.

Key findings

  • No separation of duties or least privilege: every employee could access cardholder data and PII/SPII, increasing internal threat likelihood.
  • Customer credit card data stored unencrypted in plain text, a direct PCI DSS violation with financial and reputational consequences.
  • Positives in place: a firewall with appropriately defined rules, antivirus software, and active monitoring by the IT department.

[ Incident Report · July 2026 ]

Applying the NIST CSF to a DoS Incident

Summary

At approximately 09:20, all internal network services stopped responding. Investigation showed the network was overwhelmed by a sustained flood of incoming ICMP packets, consistent with a denial of service attack. The team contained the event by blocking incoming ICMP traffic at the perimeter, taking non-critical services offline and prioritising restoration of critical services. Normal operations were disrupted for around two hours. The attacker exploited a firewall that had been left unconfigured, with no rate limiting or filtering in place.

Identify

A full audit of the network, affected systems and perimeter devices confirmed the root cause: an unconfigured firewall with no rules restricting inbound ICMP traffic. The affected assets were the internal network and every dependent service. No evidence of data access or exfiltration; the attack targeted availability, not confidentiality.

Protect

The firewall is now fully configured with a rule that rate-limits incoming ICMP packets, and source IP address verification checks for spoofed addresses. An IDS/IPS filters ICMP traffic with suspicious characteristics. Firewall configurations will be reviewed on a regular schedule and staff will receive secure configuration training.

Detect

Network monitoring software alerts on abnormal traffic patterns and ICMP volume spikes. Firewall logging has been enabled so inbound traffic can be baselined against normal daily patterns, and user account activity is tracked to flag unusual behaviour.

Respond

The plan for future incidents: isolate affected systems quickly, block offending traffic at the perimeter and shed non-critical services to preserve capacity. All incidents are reported to upper management with legal and regulatory notifications met where applicable, followed by structured post-incident analysis.

Recover

Recovery was staged: attack traffic stayed blocked while congestion cleared, critical services returned first, and non-critical services were restored last once monitoring confirmed stability. This forms the recovery playbook for future availability incidents.

Reflection: a single overlooked control, one unconfigured firewall, was enough to take the entire internal network offline for two hours. Availability attacks do not need sophisticated intrusion, only an unguarded entry point, so perimeter configuration reviews are now a standing programme item.

[ Incident Report · July 2026 ]

Brute Force Attack & Malicious Redirect

Scenario

A former employee of a recipe website brute-forced the administrative account by repeatedly entering known default passwords, embedded JavaScript that prompted visitors to download an executable disguised as a browser update, then changed the admin password. Customers who ran the file were redirected to a spoofed clone of the site containing malware, and reported slower machines to the helpdesk.

Investigation

The attack was replicated in a sandboxed virtual machine while capturing traffic with tcpdump. The trace showed the browser resolving the legitimate domain over DNS (port 52444 to the DNS server), establishing an HTTP connection on port 80 confirmed by the SYN handshake flags, then the file download. Minutes later the capture showed a second DNS resolution for the spoofed domain and a new HTTP connection from a fresh source port, confirming the downloaded file redirected users to an attacker-controlled site.

// Extract from the tcpdump trace narrative

14:18:32 · DNS A? query for the legitimate domain, answered 203.0.113.22

14:18:36 · TCP SYN / SYN-ACK to port 80, HTTP GET / HTTP/1.1

14:20:32 · New DNS A? query for the spoofed domain, answered 192.0.2.17

14:25:29 · New HTTP session to the spoofed site from source port 56378

Protocol identified

HTTP at the application layer. Both the legitimate and spoofed sites communicated over port 80, and the malicious file was transported to user machines over HTTP.

Recommendations

  • Enforce a password policy requiring longer passwords with special characters, numbers and capitals, replacing default credentials immediately.
  • Add multi factor authentication as a second layer of identity proof.
  • Limit login attempts to blunt brute force attacks at source.

[ Incident Report · July 2026 ]

Wireshark DNS Outage Investigation

Incident

Customers reported a "destination port unreachable" error when attempting to visit a website. Packet sniffing tests were conducted in Wireshark to investigate. Note: IP addresses and the domain in the working log are anonymised stand-ins so nothing traces back to a real environment.

// Pattern repeating across the capture

13:24:32 · UDP DNS A? query from the client to the DNS server (port 53)

13:24:36 · ICMP reply: udp port 53 unreachable

// Same request retried at 13:26 and 13:28, same ICMP error each time

Analysis

The UDP protocol carried the DNS query to resolve the domain, and ICMP carried the error response back. Because port 53 is the port associated with DNS, the repeated "udp port 53 unreachable" errors isolate the fault to the DNS service itself: the request never reached a working DNS listener. The A? flag on each query confirms the client was performing standard A-record resolution when the failures occurred.

Conclusion and next steps

It is highly likely the DNS server is not responding, either down due to a successful denial of service attack or misconfigured. The next investigative step is to determine whether the DNS server itself is offline or whether traffic to port 53 is being blocked by the firewall.
